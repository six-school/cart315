Taking an inventory of some thing I wanted to do that I think might be achievable. Maybe I can filter down from this:

- Introducing a musical pulse that persists as the player moves around, so that there's some continuity between regions. I think this was a big reason for prototyping the synth engine in the first place, so I'd be excited to implement it. Uh... this probably doesn't count as a mechanical change per se.

- Growing the area. It's pretty small; some areas are basically stubs. It might be fine if the prototype had substantial mechanics to explore within this area, but for a walking sim it's pretty tiny. Wouldn't be a big lift to add more areas, but probably this should follow from mechanics rather than being an "enhancement" (per the prototype brief) to itself.

- Adding doors?? Sounds kind of silly, but the way I was conceptualizing of it, it's an interaction that leans into the affordances of the synchronized audio. I mentioned it in my previous prototyping journal:

  > "Apartment doors you knock on (a drum fill) and that open on the next bar, sweeping up lowpass filter cutoff."

  In my defense, this was written before Vadim did an in-class demo prototyping doors! It's small, but... maybe it's enough? Given that I probably don't have time to make a more bombastic test of the music synchronization, this might do. And there's a kind of economy to it -- once the core interaction is implemented, I can lay down a few doors throughout the space in different contexts...

- Collecting parts of a song or piece of music by exploring an area. This should be pretty simple to implement. In my previous journal wrapup, I wrote:

  > Rather than just gradually "unmuting" pieces of audio, I think it could be cool to try, say, changing a melody and increasing its intensity by \[for example] using Euclidean sequencing and gradually adding notes.

- Doing a semi-procedural non-Euclidean rooms thing. (How many confusingly distinct meanings of "Euclidean" can I introduce in this journal entry? I mean spatial Euclideanness now, to be clear.) Implemented by spawning in and out adjacent rooms -- the non-Euclideanness comes not from any self-evident warping of space, but from the fact that you might, say, go through a doorway and turn 90 degrees, and go through another doorway and turn again, and again twice more, and not end up back where you started. I prototyped something like this a long time back and it was kind of compelling, but that was around the time of my burnout and I never came back to the idea.

  The implementation is simple: rooms can be any shape, as complex as you want, so long as the doors lie on the convex hull of the room shape. That prevents self-intersection between any two adjacent rooms. Then when you're close to another door the door you came through closes, such that you can never see more than two rooms at a time. Once a door closes, you can hide the room behind it. That's all there is to it.

  So, uh, another door-based idea. Do I have doors on the brain? Hmm... maybe this can be a stretch goal, since it feels contingent on the previous door-based idea...

Okay, that's enough prattling... I've convinced myself, I'll add doors as a mechanic. For the second "enhancement" mandated by the brief I guess I'll see if I can't introduce a pulse -- non-Euclidean rooms sounds a bit more ambitious since I'd have to model a bunch of rooms. Collecting parts of a song is pretty achievable, but the pulse/continuity of music feels like it's more essential to the whole idea.
